# DISCOVERY03 — Reverse-adjoint shared-candidate research (2026-11-04)

## Scope and mathematical contract
For a month-multiplicity tuple of positive integers, legal words are those with ordered first and ordered last occurrences. The existing verified forward transfer is T_j with entries T_j[i+1,p] = 1[p<=i] * weight_j(i), where i indexes the first positive month-j insertion gap. For a prefix and candidate c, all later-month operators j>c see c as an older letter; they therefore have the same operator for every earlier candidate. Compute reverse suffix vectors v_j = T_{j+1,older}^t ... T_{m,older}^t (1), with each transpose computed as (T_j^t v)[p] = sum_{i>=p} weight_j(i)*v[i+1]. Candidate count is dot(v_c, T_{c,self} * forward[c-1]) for c>=2, and dot(v_1, base[1]) for c=1. The candidate sum equals the current continuation count. A selected symbol updates only the affected forward suffix.

Stream reverse-adjoint vectors rather than retaining every reverse vector: this reduces peak memory compared with the first local prototype. The chosen architecture shares the entire reverse suffix computation across candidate letters. It does not yet avoid updating a full transfer chain on each of 4,244 symbols. Research only; no production Chapel modifications.

## Validation
Independent small-occurrence-poset and complete-word enumeration: 137 families, 1,982 exact rank/unrank assertions and 8,361 per-prefix partition checks passed locally. Bounded full-size median-prefix profile (45 months / 4,244 days, total 22,470 bits) executed its first six choices, compared one completed prefix count to independent direct transfer and checked exact root hash; 9.212 s and 295,700 KiB local peak RSS. Median whole-word rank/unrank was NOT completed. Earlier unbounded-adjoint prototype was stopped at 24 s (exit 124) without reaching 100 symbols and hit 569,452 KiB; this was a performance/resource diagnostic, not a mathematical counterexample.
