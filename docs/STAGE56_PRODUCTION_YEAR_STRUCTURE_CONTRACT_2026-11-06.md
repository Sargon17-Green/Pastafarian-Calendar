## Independent algorithmic choices and proof obligations
- For positive cutlet compositions without forced r, count is C(g-1,k-1). With a forced proper cut at r, count is sum over j=1..k-1 of C(r-1,j-1) C(g-r-1,k-j-1). Use these binomial block counts to unrank each lexicographic next part; do not transplant the recursive \`CutletPartitionCounter\` from tests/oracle.
- For month lengths with n slots remaining and remainder S, let q=S-4n. Coefficient of (1+z+...+z^119)^n is the exact inclusion-exclusion sum Σ[j=0..n] (-1)^j C(n,j) C(q-120j+n-1,n-1), with out-of-range binomial=0. Lexicographically subtract candidate-prefix blocks to unrank. Do not transplant \`BoundedCompositionCounter\` from tests/oracle.
- Distinct names: derive an independent mixed-radix / factoradic rank decomposition using falling factorial block sizes; use independent small permutations and catalog index bounds as invariants.
- Weaving is the unproved large-state engineering risk. Design an **independent exact production frontier-counting algorithm** with mathematically justified block counts for each admissible next month, preserving first/last occurrence order. Do not paste or line-translate the test oracle's suffix insertion DP, reopened-branch factorization, caches, or rank function. Before committing the production algorithm, document its state, recurrence, base case, ordering, exact-integer proof, complexity bound and 512MiB/900s worst-relevant-year profile. If no safe large-state algorithm has been proven, keep this subphase NOT ACCEPTED, instead of adding approximate or fixture-based results.
- Numerical examples: (g,k)=(8,3) has 21 unconstrained partitions and 6 partitions with required cut r=3. (L,m)=(15,3) with lengths 4..123 has 10 vectors; 369 at m=3 has one [123,123,123], 370 has none. For tiny legal weaving: lengths [1,1] -> 1, [2,1] ->1, [1,2]->1, [2,2]->2.
